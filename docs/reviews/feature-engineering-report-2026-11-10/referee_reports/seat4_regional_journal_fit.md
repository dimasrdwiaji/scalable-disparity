# Referee Report: Spatial Feature Engineering for Physical Disparity
**Reviewer Identity / Seat**: Southeast Asian Megacities & Comparative Urbanism / Journal-Fit
**Evaluation Date**: 2026-11-09
**Overall Recommendation**: Major Revision
---
### 1. Summary of Appraisal

The report proposes seven hexagon-level indicators of physical living conditions for Greater Jakarta. The topic is timely, the open-data premise is attractive, and the limitations section is unusually candid. In its current form, however, it is a pre-modelling technical audit and not a research article. Its two stated contributions are a definitional distinction and the use of stratification, and no baseline, independent deprivation outcome or decision use is tested. Its most striking value, about 2.9 m² per person in named kampungs, comes from the non-residential building filter and not from crowding: cells around Tambora are near 10 to 15 m². Susenas is named as ground truth, but no comparison is reported, and the one held in the repository does not validate the measure. The core-periphery amenity gap is largely density composition, and the remainder cannot be divided between real provision and data visibility. The Manila benchmark has no verifiable source, and the definitions need structural recalibration elsewhere. The manuscript also describes data sources that differ from those in the repository. All of this is repairable, and a validated second-city version could be a useful contribution. No venue was configured, so fit is judged only for the field's general readership.

### 2. Major Methodological Critiques (P0 / P1)

- **Point 1 (Conceptual Validity)**: The contribution is asserted and not demonstrated. Stewart and Oke (2012) already separate building surface fraction from impervious fraction, so the "paradox" is a clarification. Stratifying by administrative tier is standard. The manuscript needs a question, and evidence that its features add information beyond population density and a named baseline: GHSL settlement classes, the Relative Wealth Index (Chi et al., 2022), morphological slum work (Taubenböck & Kraff, 2014; Wurm & Taubenböck, 2018) or IDEAMAPS (Thomson et al., 2020). The report also conflates ground footprint with floor space. Its LCZ claim fails on its own numbers: 28.75% gross coverage sits outside the LCZ 3 range of 40 to 70% and inside the LCZ 6 range of 20 to 40%. The hazard dimension is built on slope, while flooding and subsidence dominate physical risk in north Jakarta (Abidin et al., 2011) and riverine Manila (Porio, 2011).

- **Point 2 (Data & Metric Integrity)**: I checked the headline values against repository outputs. If §5.4's 48.2% coverage and 2.92 m²/person describe one cell, the implied density is about 165,000/km², or about 122,000 people in one 74 ha cell; the largest cell in the data holds 32,059. The 11 cells around Tambora have net footprints of 9.9 to 15.0 m² per person, and Kalibaru, named as acute, has 18.5 to 20.5 m². DKI's P5 is 2.89 m² net and 11.2 m² before the filter. The 68 DKI cells below 5 m² had a median 93.6% of footprint removed as non-residential, and only 3 cells fall below 5 m² before the filter. The report's claim that footprint per capita "accurately" reveals these conditions should be withdrawn. Beyond this, the report states Google Open Buildings, OSM POIs, GHS-BUILT-C, FABDEM and a 1,125 m cutoff, while the repository uses Overture buildings and places, no GHS-BUILT-C mask, no FABDEM and a 1,000 m cutoff. DKI ΔLST is +2.78 K in the text and +10.90 K recomputed. One frozen pipeline must generate every exhibit. The Kota stratum omits Kota Tangerang, whose 242 cells sit in the Kabupaten stratum, and 870 cells (1.27 M people) fall outside every boundary polygon.

- **Point 3 (Artifacts & Confounders)**: The OSM artefact is named but untested, and it is diagnosed for the wrong dataset, since the POIs actually come from Overture Places, whose bias is commercial and platform listing. Overture's source field is dropped at ingestion, so building-source gradients cannot be examined either (compare Herfort et al., 2023). Population denominators are unreconciled: repository values for Kota Bekasi (3.30 M) and Depok (2.90 M) are above figures reported from BPS (about 2.63 M and 2.17 M, from secondary sources I could not confirm against BPS tables), whereas peak cells near Tambora reach 36,000/km² against a kecamatan-wide figure near 49,800/km². GHS-POP may compress peaks and over-allocate to satellites, which would move every per-capita contrast between strata (Leyk et al., 2019).

### 3. Minor Technical Concerns (P2)

- Slum labels on compactness persist even though §6.3 concedes the measure fails to separate kampungs from formal subdivisions. Kampungs are not synonymous with slums (Tunas & Peresthu, 2010).
- The text uses 5.0 m² while the repository's validation uses 7.2 m²; neither applies directly to cell-level ground footprint.
- There are no maps, which a study of spatial disparity needs.
- The report's §5.1 list of Kabupaten units omits Serang and Bandung Barat, and the term "Jabodetabek" is used for an area extending to Karawang and Lebak.
- Text and exhibits conflict: 83.8% against 80.9%; Kota density 6,840 against 9,143/km² from the same table; a 461.4 m hexagon edge that cannot match a 0.737 km² cell (about 532 m); and §5.4's 2.92 m² low in Jakarta Barat beside 2.84 m² in Jakarta Utara.

### 4. Fact-Checking & Comparative Grounding

*Answers to the three Seat 4 questions:*

**Q1. How do floor space per capita and amenity counts compare with Susenas and with Tondo or Baseco?**
The comparison is not reported and cannot be made as the question frames it, because 13.0 m² is ground footprint and not floor space. The DKI figure (12.97 m² population-weighted) is close to Susenas (12.27 m²), but this is agreement between different constructs. Footprint ignores storeys and Susenas is reported dwelling area. The repository's modelled floor area is 1.65 to 2.11 times Susenas in the DKI municipalities, with a rank correlation of −0.21 across units. Footprint per capita, compared by me across 14 units, has ratios of 0.73 to 1.29, and a rank correlation of −0.08 across the 9 non-DKI units. The Susenas column is itself derived, being median dwelling area divided by mean household size, and DKI's value is repeated five times. The 7.2 m² share is 22% in Susenas, 0 to 11.2% for modelled floor area in DKI municipalities, 10.3% for net footprint and 0.8% for gross footprint at DKI level. The 2.9 m² figure is unsupported.

For Manila, reported 2020 census figures give Tondo about 75,600 persons/km² (654,220 on 8.65 km²) and Baseco about 58,750/km² (64,750 on 1.102 km²); these come from secondary sources and should be checked against PSA tables. Applying an illustrative coverage of 0.40 to 0.50 gives about 5.3 to 6.6 m²/person in Tondo and 6.8 to 8.5 m² in Baseco, as district averages. Tambora, at about 49,800/km² and 0.40 coverage, gives about 8 m². A cell-level P5 of 2.9 m² would be well below those denser Manila districts' averages, which is a warning sign, and these bounds also suggest GHS-POP may be overstating footprint per capita in the densest Jakarta cells. I know of no published amenity count for Tondo or Baseco on a comparable definition, so that comparison requires running the same procedure there.

**Q2. Does the core-periphery contrast reflect real disparity or data-coverage artefacts?**
It reflects a mix, and the manuscript has no evidence to separate them. The 126 against 2 median contrast is mostly density composition, since most Kabupaten cells are low density. Within density bins, DKI and Kabupaten medians are similar below 10,000/km² (4 v 3 at 1,000 to 3,000; 6 v 7 at 3,000 to 6,000; 25 v 16 at 6,000 to 10,000). DKI keeps a 2.4 to 2.9 fold excess at 10,000 to 25,000/km² (90.5 v 31; 154 v 63.5). Network snapping does not explain the zeros. The residual could be real commercial concentration or better listing of urban businesses, and only a register comparison can tell. The contrast in building-based indicators also rests on mis-specified strata and unreconciled population.

**Q3. Can the definitions transfer without structural recalibration?**
No. A formal 4 × 20 m Vietnamese tube house has a compactness of 0.44 and a 4 × 18 m ruko 0.47, so the proposed "irregular" rule (below 0.55) flags planned narrow-plot housing. Footprint per capita reads multi-storey row housing in Bangkok and Ho Chi Minh City as crowding. Slope is close to uninformative in deltaic cities, where flood and subsidence matter. The residential filter depends on local OSM land-use coverage and Indonesian-tuned size rules. Population quality varies with census unit size, and amenity vocabularies differ, as ADR 0004 itself recognises by making them per-region. A transferable core may exist, but it has to be established by applying the pipeline elsewhere.

- *Comparative Megacity Evidence*: The single Manila benchmark cannot be verified: its DOI resolves to an unrelated paper and the cited title concerns global land susceptibility to urbanisation, not plan area in Manila. No Bangkok, Ho Chi Minh City or Surabaya evidence is offered. Relevant regional context exists for urban form and policy (Firman, 2009; Hudalah & Firman, 2012; Shatkin, 2004; Boonyabancha, 2005; Estoque et al., 2020) but is unused.
- *Empirical Literature Concordance*: The focus on morphology and plan area is consistent with deprivation-mapping practice (Kuffer et al., 2016), but several findings go against the argument made: the LCZ claim, and §6.6's use of road-network completeness evidence for POIs. At least nine DOIs in the reference list fail or resolve to unrelated work, so the regional grounding needs rebuilding. On originality and fit, no venue was configured and I make no venue-alignment claim. For a general urban analytics readership the current contribution is insufficient. A validated, comparative version would be of interest to that readership; a remote-sensing readership would expect a measurement advance not present here.

### 5. Concrete Actionable Recommendations for Revision

1. State a research question and demonstrate added value against population density and at least one named baseline, with an independent deprivation outcome such as BPS RW Kumuh or Susenas microdata.
2. Regenerate every number from one frozen pipeline, and make the methods, data and results describe it. Remove §7 from any submission.
3. Withdraw the 2.9 m² settlement claims, validate the non-residential filter, and report footprint and floor area separately against Susenas with appropriate construct caveats.
4. Test coverage directly: validate amenity counts against Podes, Ministry of Health facility data and Dapodik by kecamatan or desa, keep Overture source metadata, and report completeness by stratum.
5. Correct the study area and strata from official boundaries, and reconcile GHS-POP against kecamatan and desa census counts with sensitivity to population source.
6. Apply the unchanged pipeline to at least one peer city, ideally Metro Manila with Tondo and Baseco included, and separate universal from locally calibrated definitions. Replace or justify compactness for shophouse and tube-house forms.
7. Add flood and subsidence hazards, or narrow the hazard construct.
8. Separate formal-service from everyday amenity measures and add a demand-sensitive form.
9. Rebuild the bibliography from works actually read, and add indicator, strata and missingness maps.
