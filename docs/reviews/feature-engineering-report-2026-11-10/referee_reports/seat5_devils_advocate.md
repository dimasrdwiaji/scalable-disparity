# Referee Report: Spatial Feature Engineering for Physical Disparity
**Reviewer Identity / Seat**: Devil's Advocate
**Evaluation Date**: 2026-11-09
**Overall Recommendation**: Major Revision
---
### 1. Summary of Appraisal
The strongest counter-argument is that this feature store mostly records how built-up a place is, several times over. Using the repository's own matrix, density and distance to Monas predict 59.1% of scaled feature variance in spatially blocked cross-validation, and the first principal component, holding 70.1% of variance, at R-squared 0.83. Density does nearly all of this work; Monas adds little. Two problems block acceptance. The headline 2.8 to 3.1 m2 per person "overcrowding" comes from the non-residential filter: the DKI lowest-5% cells lost a median 99.5% of their footprint, and cells around Tambora show 7.9 to 15.0 m2. Second, footprint per capita does not eliminate dasymetric circularity at cell scale, because GHS-POP is almost entirely rescaled built volume (R-squared 0.976). The work has real merit. It documents its own uncertainties candidly, signal survives inside DKI (heat is nearly independent of density there), and footprint per capita agrees with Susenas in magnitude at census-unit scale. Both Critical defects can be repaired by narrowing claims and new analysis. On the three questions: partly a density proxy, yes on circularity though by a different route than posed, and mixed on actionability, which the paper never addresses.

### 2. Major Methodological Critiques (P0 / P1)
- **Point 1 (Conceptual Validity)**: *Seat 5 Question 1, the Proxy Fallacy: largely yes at metropolitan scale, only partly inside the core.* Method: cubic terms in log density and distance to Monas with an interaction, 5-fold cross-validation blocked on H3 resolution 5 parents, 8,375 modelled cells. Out-of-block R-squared: plan area 0.81, amenity 0.76, NDVI 0.61, heat 0.58, terrain 0.17, compactness 0.10, space sufficiency 0.00. Jointly 59.1% of scaled variance (43.1% z-scored). Predicted and true inter-cell distances correlate at 0.72. Distance adds little beyond density (plan area 0.818 on density alone, 0.821 with both). The channels that escape the proxy do so because they carry little variation: space sufficiency is saturated at 1.0 in 76.3% of cells, compactness spans 0.70 to 0.84, and terrain safety is at 1.0 in 59.5% of cells. Within DKI the fit weakens: plan area 0.42, amenity 0.41, NDVI 0.22, heat 0.02, PC1 R-squared 0.53. Density is a legitimate physical attribute, and correlation alone does not make an indicator invalid, so I rate this Major (M1). The fault is the claim of distinctness made without a test. Disparity is also undefined (M2): amenity runs opposite to plan area (-0.80), NDVI (-0.66), and heat (-0.66), so an equal-weight composite is almost flat across density deciles (0.712 to 0.676), while a variance-seeking encoder would follow the density axis. Composite construction norms (OECD/JRC Handbook, 2008) expect the aggregation to be stated and tested.

  *Seat 5 Question 3, Actionability: mixed, and unaddressed (M4).* Modifiable through programmes: vegetation, surface heat, amenity supply. Modifiable only through redevelopment or relocation: plan area, space per person. Fixed or uninformative: slope, a geographic given whose marginalisation rationale is untested (M5), and compactness, which has no lever and by the paper's own account does not discriminate (M9). Lowering plan area also conflicts with the proximity the amenity channel rewards. A 74 ha hexagon is not a planning unit. The interventions that kampung upgrading actually delivered in Indonesia, such as paving, drainage, and water (Harari and Wong 2026), do not appear among the indicators. Planners would get a useful descriptive screen for greening, cooling, and service gaps, but not a set of leverage points. Slope and other fixed context should be separated from the disparity score.

- **Point 2 (Data & Metric Integrity)**: *C1, the flagship overcrowding figure is a filter artefact.* The DKI P5 value reproduces (2.89 m2), so the number is real but its meaning is not. The 44 cells in that tail lost a median 99.5% of footprint to the non-residential filter; before filtering their median is 25.9 m2, after filtering 0.21 m2. Only one is in Jakarta Barat, while 24 are in Jakarta Utara. DKI cells with under 20% removal have P5 of 10.5 m2. Of DKI cells below 5 m2, 95.6% lost over half their footprint, and before filtering only 0.35% of DKI cells are below 5 m2. The nine cells within 1.5 km of Tambora range from 7.9 to 15.0 m2. One Penjaringan-area cell reaches 2.72 m2 after losing 92.7% of its footprint. Radii are approximate because no kecamatan boundaries were available. Either population is misplaced in non-residential cells or the filter removes residences; neither is evidence of kampung crowding. Separately, ground footprint is compared against a living-area threshold (M10), which ignores storeys.

  Report tables are not a single reproducible result (M7). Kota density is inconsistent with the table's own population and area (9,143, not 6,840 per km2). Against current outputs, population is 32.10 M versus 40.48 M, Kabupaten heat changes sign, and the within-DKI heat ranking changes. Exclusions also fall on featured places (M11): 8,375 of 9,345 cells are modelled, with 15.6% of DKI population excluded and LST failure above 32% in Jakarta Utara and Barat. The described pipeline diverges from the code, so the reader cannot tell which is authoritative. In particular, the matrix still uses the volume-based channel that §6.2 condemns (M3). I treat this as a finding because, whichever document is stale, the stated repair does not reach the object going into modelling.

- **Point 3 (Artifacts & Confounders)**: *Seat 5 Question 2, the Circularity Trap: yes, though not through the route posed (C2).* The repository's footprints are Overture, not Google Open Buildings directly, and I could not confirm whether Google footprints trained any GHSL layer: GHSL product pages do not state it, and the Data Package and Pesaresi et al. (2024) full text could not be retrieved. The coupling does not need that route. Log population on log residential volume has R-squared 0.976, consistent with the allocation the report itself describes. Footprint per capita factors exactly into filter retention, footprint relative to GHS volume, and GHS volume per person. The last term is the only one carrying census information, and it accounts for -0.2% of log variance overall and 1.5% within DKI. The filter accounts for 59.3% overall and 103.9% within DKI. Volume per person is nearly constant between neighbours (correlation 0.965, median difference 0.007 m2), the plateau expected of a unit-level allocation constant. At cell scale the indicator is therefore a ratio between building products modified by a filter, and not an "empirical, model-free" measure. It is also algebraically plan area divided by density (Kronmal 1993 on ratio coupling). At census-unit scale it is informative, which fixes the boundary of valid inference.

### 3. Minor Technical Concerns (P2)
- The 48.2% maximum plan area lies in Jakarta Utara, not the named Jakarta Barat kampungs (Barat maximum 0.451).
- The peri-urban share is given as both 83.8% and 80.9%.
- The repository's universe is selected at 300 persons per km2, which conditions the sample on the proxy being examined, whereas the report states a Pop of at least 50.
- The "essential" POI set includes restaurants, cafes, ATMs, and banks, which weakens its reading as service equity.
- Stratum composition is inconsistent with §5.1: 242 Kota Tangerang cells fall in the Kabupaten stratum, along with 870 cells outside every polygon.

### 4. Fact-Checking & Comparative Grounding
- *Comparative Megacity Evidence*: The claimed alignment with LCZ 3 and Metro Manila is not supported (M8). DKI's 21.88 to 28.75% sits below the LCZ 3 range of 40 to 70% and within LCZ 6 at 20 to 40% (Stewart and Oke 2012). The 40 to 48% "cap" is unsourced and matches this study's own maximum. Some dilution at 74 ha is plausible but must be computed. The cited Manila source does not resolve to a matching paper, and I have no verified Manila plan-area figure to substitute. Estoque, Murayama and Myint (2017, Science of the Total Environment 577, 349-359) do provide comparative evidence on land composition and surface temperature in Bangkok, Jakarta, and Manila, which suggests the heat-vegetation-built coupling here is regional. That work is not a plan-area benchmark.
- *Empirical Literature Concordance*: Three references could not be traced and several DOIs point to unrelated works (M12). The real Taubenböck, Kraff and Wurm (2018) paper is in Applied Geography 92, 150-167, and Kuffer, Pfeffer and Sliuzas (2016) is a review titled "Slums from Space", which also shows that this literature reports accuracy against reference data, something absent here (M6). Lilford et al. (2019, BMJ Global Health 4, e001267) and Thomson et al. (doi 10.20944/preprints201910.0242.v3) frame deprived areas in ways that go beyond built morphology. At unit level, footprint per capita is 0.74 to 1.26 of Susenas (Spearman 0.56 over 16 units with six DKI units sharing one value; 0.39 over 10 non-DKI units), better than the pipeline's modelled floor area (Spearman -0.21). Unit-level concordance does not validate cell-level values.

### 5. Concrete Actionable Recommendations for Revision
1. Withdraw the Tambora and Penjaringan 2.8 to 3.1 m2 claim. Report footprint per capita before and after filtering, and flag cells where the filter removes most of the footprint.
2. Retract "eliminates" and "model-free". Use population not allocated by built layers at sub-unit scale, such as official kelurahan counts, or limit inference to census-unit scale.
3. Add a density-and-distance baseline, by channel and stratum, as a required benchmark for any index built on this feature store.
4. Define how channels combine into disparity, and test alternative directions and weights before modelling.
5. Resolve which channel 1 is, and align the report with the code. Remove or replace compactness and show that any replacement separates known groups.
6. Classify each indicator as modifiable, partly modifiable, or fixed context, tie it to a planning instrument at a planning-relevant unit, and keep fixed context out of the disparity score.
7. Present Susenas comparisons in full and add known-group validation against mapped kampung and formal areas.
8. Regenerate all tables from one tagged run, report the modelled universe, test sensitivity to missing LST, and verify every reference.
