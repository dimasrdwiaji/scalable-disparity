## Review Body

Strongest counter-argument. The most parsimonious reading of the feature store is that it describes one thing, how built-up a place is, and then describes that thing several more times. Across the 8,375 modelled cells, a cubic model of log density and distance to Monas, scored by 5-fold cross-validation blocked on H3 resolution 5 parents, recovers 59.1% of scaled feature variance and 43.1% when channels are z-scored. The first principal component carries 70.1% of scaled variance, and density plus distance predicts it with out-of-block R-squared 0.83. Pairwise cell distances in the true space correlate 0.72 with those in a space predicted from density and distance alone (Mantel, 2,500 cells). The paper never runs this comparison.

Question 1, the Proxy Fallacy: largely yes at metropolitan scale, only partly inside the core. Out-of-block R-squared by channel is plan area 0.81, amenity 0.76, NDVI 0.61, heat 0.58, terrain 0.17, compactness 0.10, space sufficiency 0.00. Distance adds little beyond density (plan area 0.818 on density alone, 0.821 with both), so the operative proxy is density, and Monas is mostly redundant. The three channels that escape the proxy do not escape because they measure something independent: space sufficiency is saturated at 1.0 in 76.3% of modelled cells, compactness spans only 0.70 to 0.84 between P1 and P99 after scaling, and terrain safety is at 1.0 in 59.5% of cells. Fairness requires the converse. Within DKI the same model gives plan area 0.42, amenity 0.41, NDVI 0.22, heat 0.02, and PC1 explains 36% of within-DKI variance with R-squared 0.53. Administrative strata alone explain 21% of z-scored variance. So there is real intra-core signal, heat especially, and density is itself a defensible physical attribute. That is why this is Major and not Critical. The paper's error is claiming independence without testing it.

Question 2, the Circularity Trap: yes, though not through the route the question names. Log GHS-POP on log GHS-BUILT-V residential volume has R-squared 0.976 across 9,260 cells, consistent with the report's own statement that population is allocated in proportion to volume. Footprint per capita factors exactly as (net footprint / footprint before filtering) x (footprint / GHS volume) x (GHS volume / population). The last term, the only one carrying census information, accounts for -0.2% of log footprint-per-capita variance overall and 1.5% within DKI. The non-residential filter term accounts for 59.3% overall and 103.9% within DKI; sensor disagreement between footprint and volume products accounts for the rest. At cell scale the indicator is therefore a filter-and-product-ratio statistic. Volume per person has a neighbour correlation of 0.965 with a median neighbour difference of 0.007 m2 per person, which is the plateau pattern of a unit-level allocation constant. I could not confirm whether Google footprints trained any GHSL layer: the GHSL product pages do not state it, and the Data Package and Pesaresi et al. (2024) full text could not be retrieved. The repository's footprints are Overture, so the question's premise needs restating, but the demonstrated coupling does not depend on it.

A credit that bounds this finding: aggregated to Susenas units, population-weighted median footprint per capita sits at 0.74 to 1.26 of the Susenas median floor area per capita (Spearman 0.56 over 16 units with six DKI units sharing one value; 0.39 over the 10 non-DKI units). That beats the pipeline's modelled floor area (ratio up to 2.11, Spearman -0.21). Footprint mass is informative at census-unit scale. The defect is at sub-unit scale, which is the analysis scale.

Question 3, Actionability: mixed, and the paper does not address it. Vegetation, surface heat, and amenity supply are changeable by programmes. Slope is a geographic given. Compactness has no policy meaning and, by the paper's own account, no discriminating power. Plan area and space per person cannot be moved without relocation or redevelopment, and lowering plan area conflicts with the proximity the amenity channel rewards. Oriented so that higher is better, amenity correlates with plan area at -0.80 and with NDVI and heat at -0.66. An equal-weight composite is nearly flat, falling only from 0.712 to 0.676 across density deciles with SD 0.037, and DKI (0.688) scores barely below the Kabupaten stratum (0.706). The seven channels thus support either a density axis, if the encoder keeps the dominant variance direction, or a near-constant, if channels are averaged. The levers Indonesian kampung upgrading actually pulled, such as paving, drainage, and water (Harari and Wong 2026, Review of Economic Studies 93, 2646-2679), are absent.

Minor items, not tabled: the 48.2% maximum plan area is in Jakarta Utara, not the named Jakarta Barat kampungs (Barat maximum 0.451); 83.8% and 80.9% are both given for the peri-urban share; the residential universe is selected on density at 300 per km2 in the repository, which conditions the sample on the proxy; the "essential" POI set includes restaurants, cafes, ATMs, and banks.
