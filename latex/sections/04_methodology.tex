\section{Methodology}

Section~4 specifies the analytical pipeline designed to answer \textbf{RQ2} and \textbf{RQ3}: defining a physical urban analysis mask and local thermal reference area, evaluating each vetted variable individually, testing arithmetic versus penalty-adjusted domain synthesis alongside hexagonal inequality metrics, and benchmarking Spatial GAT-AE boundary detection against classical spatial baselines.

\subsection{Urban Analysis Mask and Local Thermal Influence Area}
To prevent rural farmland, uninhabited industrial parks, or population-allocation assumptions from entering neighborhood scoring, cells enter the intra-urban analysis strictly through physical land-cover criteria: a hexagon must belong to an urban settlement class in \texttt{GHS-SMOD} ($\{21, 22, 23, 30\}$) and contain at least one residential building footprint remaining after removing OpenStreetMap non-residential polygons. No gridded population cutoff (\texttt{GHS-POP} or WorldPop) is used to include or weight cells.

Within this urban mask, intra-urban surface temperature anomaly ($\Delta\text{LST}_i$) is defined relative to nearby urban peers rather than distant forests or a single citywide average. Because citywide medians reflect macro coastal cooling gradients, valley elevation slopes, and the overall balance of slums versus estates across an entire metropolis, neighborhood disparity is measured against a \textbf{local $k=5$ H3 urban ring} ($\approx 2.6\text{ km}$ radius around cell $i$), computed strictly across urban-masked neighbor cells $j \in \mathcal{N}_5^{\text{urban}}(i)$:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta\text{LST}_i = \text{LST}_i - \operatorname{median}_{j \in \mathcal{N}_5^{\text{urban}}(i)} \left(\text{LST}_j\right).
\end{equation}
Comparing each cell against urban neighborhoods within roughly 2.6~km removes regional coastal and elevation gradients across both coastal Jakarta and upland cities such as Bandung while retaining micro-scale thermal contrast between adjacent formal estates and crowded \textit{kampungs} \citep{Stewart2012}. Citywide urban-cluster medians \citep{Chakraborty2019a, Hsu2021} are computed solely as a sensitivity check.

\subsection{Individual Indicator Evaluation and Candidate Promotion (RQ2)}
Before constructing any composite surface, every retained and candidate indicator (\texttt{res\_share\_lt50}, \texttt{res\_nn\_p25}, \texttt{ghs\_res\_share}, \texttt{s2\_res\_glcm\_*}, \texttt{ndvi}, and \texttt{dlst\_intra}) is evaluated on its own merits across both quantitative discrimination and qualitative spatial behavior:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Binary discrimination and severity grading:} Evaluate ROC-AUC separating official \textit{Pergub~90} slum units from formal neighborhoods in Mainland DKI Jakarta, plus Spearman rank correlation ($\rho_s$) across ordered severity tiers (\textit{Kategori}~1--4) to test monotonic sensitivity to settlement consolidation.
    \item \textbf{Density independence and qualitative inspection:} Check correlation against overall building coverage alongside spatial maps examining where each indicator concentrates across historic city cores, gated enclaves, and peri-urban transitions.
\end{enumerate}

Prospective and conditional channels---specifically residential-masked 10~m Sentinel-2 GLCM texture (\texttt{s2\_res\_glcm\_*}) and \texttt{ghs\_res\_share}---promote into the joint modeling stack only if they satisfy three explicit screening gates: (i) statistically significant separation on Mainland DKI \textit{Pergub~90} neighborhoods ($p < 0.01$), (ii) low correlation with overall building plan-area coverage ($|\rho| < 0.35$) so they add signal beyond gross density, and (iii) consistent directional sign across spatially blocked validation folds.

\subsection{Domain Synthesis Test and Hexagonal Urban Inequality (RQ2)}
Validated variables are oriented so higher values consistently indicate worse physical or environmental deprivation and scaled onto positive frozen bounds $z_{ik} > 0$ matching international cross-city scaling principles, with equal weights assigned to variables inside the \textbf{Built Form} domain ($B_i$) and \textbf{Micro-Environment} domain ($E_i$).

Rather than assuming a penalty-adjusted formula improves separation without empirical verification, Stage~4 tests two aggregation forms directly against one another:
\begin{equation}
    M_i = \frac{B_i + E_i}{2}, \qquad \text{MPI}_i^+ = M_i + \frac{S_i^2}{M_i},
\end{equation}
where $S_i$ denotes the standard deviation across cell $i$'s domain scores $B_i$ and $E_i$ \citep{Mazziotta2016, OECD2008}. Because higher values signify worse conditions, adding the imbalance term $S_i^2 / M_i$ ($\text{MPI}_i^+$) increases the composite deprivation score when a neighborhood exhibits severe housing or heat stress alongside favorable values in the other domain (preventing peripheral tree cover from offsetting cramped building form). Retaining $\text{MPI}_i^+$ over the plain domain mean $M_i$ depends empirically on whether penalty adjustment improves known-group separation across DKI \textit{Pergub~90} and density-stratified Karawang.

To summarize citywide physical inequality across all urban-masked hexagons without introducing population-grid circularity, we adapt the spatial inequality suite from \texttt{greenR} \citep{Mahajan2024} and spatial Gini decomposition \citep{Rey2013}. Hexagonal Gini coefficients, Lorenz curves, and the Spatial Palma Ratio (the upper 10\% share divided by the lowest 40\% share across urban-masked cells) are computed unweighted across equal-area $0.737\text{ km}^2$ hexagons for both separate domain scores and the composite surface, using masked residential building count as an auxiliary robustness check. Because dispersion curves summarize spatial spread rather than identifying deprived residents directly, all inequality readings are interpreted strictly alongside official known-group validations.

\subsection{Benchmarked GAT-AE Boundary Detection and Transferability (RQ3)}
To detect sharp discontinuities where formal housing borders informal settlements, all $D$ individual vetted physical indicators ($D \in \{4,5,6\}$ depending on candidate promotion) are passed directly as node attributes $X \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times D}$ on the pure geometric H3 adjacency graph $A$ (retaining edges across walls, rivers, and roads). The Spatial Graph Attention Autoencoder \citep{Salehi2020, Velickovic2018} uses two stacked graph-attention encoder layers to compress $X$ into a strict two-dimensional latent bottleneck $Z \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d}$ ($d = 2$) before reconstructing $\hat{X}$ via a symmetric decoder:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \|x_i - \hat{x}_i\|_2^2 + \lambda \, \mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}}\!\left(A, \sigma(Z Z^\top)\right).
\end{equation}
Keeping $d = 2 < D$ is critical: matching latent capacity to input dimensionality ($d = D$) would allow identity copying with near-zero reconstruction error everywhere, whereas $d = 2$ enforces smooth neighborhood interpolation so abrupt formal--informal interfaces produce localized spikes in node reconstruction error $e_i = \|x_i - \hat{x}_i\|_2^2$. A $d = 3$ bottleneck is tested only as an ablation when $D \ge 5$ (and never $d = D$). Following attributed network anomaly decomposition \citep{Ding2019}, node friction is decomposed post-hoc into Built-Form Friction ($e_i^{\text{built}}$) and Micro-Environmental Friction ($e_i^{\text{env}}$), paired with learned first-layer edge attention friction $f_{ij} = 1 - \alpha_{ij}$.

To prevent spatial autocorrelation leakage and avoid unverified claims of deep-learning superiority, all boundary evaluations run over \textbf{spatially contiguous block folds} \citep{Roberts2017}. Along verified boundary edges separating \textit{Pergub~90} informal cells from adjacent formal cells in Mainland DKI Jakarta, GAT-AE node and edge friction scores are benchmarked head-to-head via boundary ROC-AUC and Precision-Recall AUC against two interpretable baselines: (1) univariate and multivariate Local Indicators of Spatial Association (LISA / Local Moran's $I$; \citealt{Anselin1995}), and (2) raw pairwise Euclidean feature differences $\|x_i - x_j\|_2$ across neighboring cells. If GAT-AE does not outperform these baselines across blocked folds, it is reported candidly rather than tuned post-hoc. Finally, cross-regime and out-of-region transferability are evaluated across density-stratified Karawang terciles (accounting explicitly for the 2.6~ha RT versus 73.7~ha H3 unit mismatch) and an external Indonesian hold-out city (Bandung or Surabaya, selected by municipal slum polygon completeness).
